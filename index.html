Gravy Math

The pan has two tablespoons of drippings, and the gravy was always going to be two cups.

Boundary: illustrative arithmetic only Gravy Math keeps labeled-norm arithmetic only. The tablespoon-for-a-cup ratio, the third-of-a-cup pour and the fix doses are labeled norms, not measurements of your pan, your stock or your guests. Taste as you go, trust your own spoon over anything this page says, and cook the flour long enough.

The roux truth

A tablespoon of fat asks a tablespoon of flour and thickens a cup of liquid. Two tablespoons of drippings were never going to make six cups.

The crowd truth

Eight guests at an honest third of a cup each ask 2.7 cups of gravy - 2.7 tablespoons of fat and flour. The bird decides the roux, not the hope.

The fix truth

Thin gravy asks a teaspoon of cornstarch per cup, whisked into cold water. Thick gravy loosens with two tablespoons of warm broth per cup. Both fixes were always this small.
